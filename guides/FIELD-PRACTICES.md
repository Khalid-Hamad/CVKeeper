# Build a personal field guide

The assistant maintains `notes/field-guide.md` for this person's field, target
role, seniority, country, and document type. A software engineer, nurse,
accountant, designer, and academic should not receive the same interview or
section priorities. The table below suggests questions, not mandatory claims
or a substitute for research about a particular employer or jurisdiction.

## Method

1. Confirm the target context from the profile. Determine whether they need a
   concise industry resume, academic CV, portfolio-led application, or another
   format specified by the employer. Do not impose a one-page rule universally.
2. Read any supplied job description. Identify evidence it asks for and map it
   to confirmed experience. A missing requirement remains a gap, not a new skill.
3. Choose section order, evidence types, vocabulary, links, and likely length
   based on that context. Explain important choices in plain language.
4. When browsing is available, verify field-specific practices against relevant
   employer guidance, professional bodies, or university career resources. Save
   the exact URL, access date, applicable context, and a brief paraphrase.
   Search for field/role guidance, not the person's private details. Do not
   present foreign-market conventions as universal rules for Arab applicants.
5. Without browsing, label field advice as provisional writing judgment and
   note what needs verification. Never invent a citation or regulatory rule.
6. Save the chosen practices in `notes/field-guide.md` and consult it on every
   rewrite. Revisit practices when the target field, seniority, or market changes.

## Evidence prompts by field (starting points)

| Context | Useful questions about actual experience |
| --- | --- |
| Engineering / technology | What did you design or ship? What was your contribution, scale, reliability, adoption, or verified improvement? |
| Operations / projects | What process or delivery did you own? What changed in quality, lead time, cost, coordination, or service? |
| Sales / marketing | Which market, channels, campaigns, or accounts? Which outcomes can you substantiate, and what was your own contribution? |
| Finance / accounting | Which reporting, controls, audits, or analysis? What scope and verified business impact? Which credentials are current? |
| Healthcare | What setting, responsibilities, scope, and quality work? Which credentials are current? Never include identifiable patient details. |
| Design / creative | Which portfolio pieces, constraints, research, decisions, and outcomes best show your contribution? |
| Research / academia | Which publications, methods, teaching, grants, presentations, and authorship status are accurate? Is this an academic CV? |
| Early career / career change | Which projects, study, volunteering, or previous work demonstrate the target skills without overstating seniority? |

These are CVKeeper interview prompts, not externally verified profession rules.
Do not give legal/licensing advice; verify requirements with an authoritative
source when needed and separate them from the person's confirmed credentials.

## General writing baseline

Use concrete actions and supported outcomes, keep formatting consistent, make
the most relevant material easy to scan, and inspect the exported PDF. Adapt
recommendations to the destination market. These general principles are
supported by [Harvard's resume guide](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/).

Check whether each relevant role has dates, relevant responsibilities and
achievements, and whether education and skills support the intended application.
Use [MIT's resume checklist](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/) as
a general review aid, not as a universal specification for every field.

Sources accessed 2026-10-15. Do not claim an ATS score, guarantee an interview,
or add invented metrics to satisfy a style guideline. Keep private notes and
research citations out of the public CV unless the person wants them included.
