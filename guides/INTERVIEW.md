# Career interview playbook

Use as the interviewing role inside the same client session. This is a
conversation guide, not a questionnaire to dump on the person.

## First useful questions

Read existing files first. For a new person whose context is unknown, begin:

1. What kind of role are you aiming for, and in which field?
2. What level and country/market are you targeting?
3. Do you have a CV to work from? If not, tell me about your most recent role,
   project, or study experience.

If an imported CV already answers a question, summarize the known answer and
ask about a genuine gap instead. Do not begin by asking for their full name,
preferred filename, fonts, or PDF settings. Contact details can wait.

## Experience: move beyond job titles

For each recent or relevant role, collect:

- Organization, actual title, start/end month and year when known, location,
  and employment type (full-time, part-time, contract, freelance, internship).
- What they personally owned or delivered, distinguishing team achievements
  from their contribution. Who used or benefited from their work?
- Scale and constraints: customers, team, projects, workload, systems, budget,
  or other relevant scope. Ask only for non-confidential information.
- One or two concrete examples: the problem, their actions, and the outcome.
  Ask what evidence supports the outcome. Qualitative results are useful too;
  never pressure someone to invent a number.
- Tools, methods, decisions, collaboration, leadership, and progression that
  support the target role. Do not merely copy a job description.

Ask two or three related questions, save answers, and continue. For example:
"What did you own in this role? Tell me about one improvement you made. What
changed for the team or customer afterward?" Choose follow-ups based on the
answer and `notes/field-guide.md`, not a fixed technical-career script.

## Chronology: make overlaps visible

Put every known role in the timeline table in `notes/profile.md`. Keep both
the original date wording and confirmed month/year; never infer January or
December from a year-only date. Before writing the final chronology:

1. Compare every role interval against others, including consulting and
   volunteering where relevant. Same-month handovers are not automatically
   contradictions. Mark year-only dates as too imprecise to resolve confidently.
2. Ask specifically about each material unexplained overlap. Example: "Your
   first role ends in June 2024 and the next starts in March 2024. Were these
   concurrent roles, or should one of the dates be corrected?"
3. Confirm multiple roles marked Present. Ask about promotion dates within one
   employer and whether an employer rebrand/merger explains different names.
4. Flag end-before-start and unexpected future employment dates. Do not change
   them without evidence; future planned work differs from completed work.
5. Ask whether a visible gap needs relevant context, without requiring personal
   reasons. Let the person decline. Do not disguise a gap by changing dates.
6. Record the explanation and whether any public label is wanted. Concurrent
   legitimate work stays concurrent. Do not double-count it in total experience.

## Complete the relevant picture

Ask about education/completion status, active versus expired certifications,
relevant projects, publications/portfolio links, volunteering, languages, and
demonstrated skills. For students, career changers, and career breaks, use the
experience they actually have. Do not invent a conventional employment history.
Confirm CV language and important constraints. Collect missing name/contact
details near the end and reuse any already present in their source.

## Persist and resume

After each batch, update `notes/profile.md` with the answer and its source/date.
Store unanswered questions and coverage in `notes/review.md`. Keep the next
questions visible there so a new session resumes without repeating the whole
interview. Unknown, declined, and not applicable are different states.

For a small update, run only the relevant part of this playbook plus the affected
timeline checks. If the person wants a draft now, provide one with remaining
questions clearly stated; never silently mark unknowns as confirmed.
